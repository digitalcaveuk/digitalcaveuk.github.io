How much does it cost to maintain a website? Start with what breaks

By Rocky V, Head of Operations at DigitalCave · 25 November 2026

The short answer: for most small business sites the running cost is a handful of small, predictable items: the domain, hosting, a security certificate, software updates and someone checking it works. The real cost is what happens when nobody owns those jobs. When we checked 1,867 small business websites across the West Midlands in October 2026, 382 of them (about 1 in 5) no longer loaded, and the most common reason was a domain that had not been renewed.

People usually ask about maintenance as a monthly figure. It is more useful to ask what you are paying to prevent. Here is how the bill breaks down, and the cheap habits that protect it.

The five lines on a yearly maintenance bill

  1. Domain renewal. Small, yearly, and the single most expensive thing to forget. In our check, 211 sites pointed at a domain that no longer existed. Turn on auto-renew and make sure the card on the registrar account has not expired.
  2. Hosting. The server your site lives on. Cheap plans are fine for a brochure site, as long as someone gets the renewal and outage emails.
  3. Security certificate (HTTPS). Usually included with hosting now. 109 of the working sites we checked still had no HTTPS, which makes browsers warn visitors away.
  4. Updates and backups. WordPress, themes and plugins need regular updates, and a backup you have actually tested. This is where most of the hands-on time goes.
  5. Checks and small fixes. Someone opening the site, testing the forms and fixing small problems before customers notice them.

A 10-minute monthly check you can do yourself

  • Open the site on your phone using mobile data, not office wi-fi.
  • Send yourself a message through the contact form and make sure it arrives.
  • Look at the footer year and your opening hours. 123 working sites in our check still showed a copyright year of 2023 or older, which makes a business look closed.
  • Log in to your domain registrar and check the renewal date and payment card.
  • Note anything odd in one place, so whoever fixes it has a list.

Who should own what

Keep the domain, hosting and Google accounts in the business's name, with your web person added as a user. Many of the dead sites we found had not been hacked; the domain simply lapsed after a change of supplier and nobody noticed. The UK National Cyber Security Centre's small business guide covers the backup and update basics in more depth.

Doing it yourself or handing it over

If you have someone organised in the business, the checks above are enough for a simple site. Hand it over when the site takes bookings or payments, when updates have broken things before, or when nobody has time to check monthly.
